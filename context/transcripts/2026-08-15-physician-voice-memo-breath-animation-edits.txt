First, I imagine the animation to be similar to the Essential Anatomy 3D app.

The entire animation should oscillate, so the muscles and ribs should expand.

The breath should include the layers of the pelvic floor, as well as
demonstrate the muscles that relax during inhalation and the ones that
contract during exhalation.

So, while the diaphragm relaxes, the rectus contracts. The transversus always
maintains tone, and the breath starts with contracting the pelvic floor and
pulling the diaphragm down, squishing the abdominal contents closer together,
while the most external layers of the body's muscles — such as the traps and
rhomboids — relax to let the thoracic cavity expand.

Relaxation and activation can be animated in movement with the heat-signature
animation that is seen in the YouTube video by Paul G., "Schrödinger's
Equation Animation."

The clinical definition of the action of the individual muscles can be used to
frame the coordination of movement and action of breathing.

Starting with engaging the pelvic floor, the levators can be seen; the
transversus; internal and external obliques; the intercostal muscles; the
scalenes of the neck; and the platysma.

Those can be seen relaxing — so, like, turning blue — while the muscles being
activated are turning red, and…
